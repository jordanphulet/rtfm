I will say this once, plainly, without a single theatrical flourish: **a guest who wants out must get out, immediately, and without being mocked.**

Good. Now, back to the theatre.

## Fun fear and real fear

Fun fear has a shape. It spikes, it breaks into laughter, it moves forward. Real fear is flat and stuck. The guest freezes, goes silent, sits down, starts to breathe too fast, or says — in a voice with no play left in it — that they want to leave.

A good haunt is designed to produce the first, and to notice the second within seconds.

## The safe word

Every guest is taught one phrase at the entrance. Ours is **“lights on”**. It is short, unlikely to come up by accident, and works shouted or whispered.

When any microphone in the house hears it:

1. The room’s stage manager stops every cue and returns every actuator to rest.
2. Work lights come up to full in that room.
3. The performer plays its pre-recorded, out-of-character line in an ordinary human voice: “You’re okay. Someone is coming to walk you out.”
4. A crew member is paged with the room name and arrives *on foot*. Never by radio voice alone.
5. The Director is *told*, after the fact. It is not asked.

This sequence runs in the stage manager’s own firmware. The safe word is matched by the room’s local speech recogniser, with no network and no language model anywhere in the path.

## Signals that mean stop, even without the word

Not everyone will remember the phrase. Treat each of these as a stop, too, and send crew to check:

- A fear level of 3 (see [Reading the Group](section:reading-the-group)) that lasts more than ten seconds.
- Anyone sitting or lying on the floor outside a planned spot.
- Phrases such as “I can’t breathe”, “please stop” or “I need to get out”.
- A child separated from their group.

False alarms will happen. A crew member who walks in to find a guest merely tying a shoelace costs you one scare. The opposite mistake costs you far more than that.

## Turning the house down

Sometimes the right answer is not to stop but to soften. A group at fear level 3 that hasn’t used the safe word gets the *gentle path* for the rest of the house:

- No strikes; only lures and releases.
- Performers switch to their kind lines. Every character has some — see [The Cast](section:the-cast).
- Room lights stay at a low but readable level.

> The best compliment I ever received came from a woman who left halfway through. She said the ghost walked her out like a gentleman.

## Pre-show checklist

- [ ] Say the safe word in every room, and watch the lights come up
- [ ] Confirm every room can page crew
- [ ] Confirm every performer has kind lines
- [ ] Walk every crew member along every walk-out route

The final word on stopping belongs to the hardware, not the software. See [The Show Must Stop](section:the-show-must-stop).
