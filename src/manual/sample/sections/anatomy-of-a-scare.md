Lay the scare on the slab. Gloves on. Let us see what it is made of.

Every scare that has ever worked — from the rubber bat on a fishing line to the most expensive dark ride on earth — has the same four organs. Learn them, and you will know what to hand to the machine and what to keep for yourself.

## The four organs

1. **The lure.** Something draws the eye. A sound off to the left, a light flickering in a doorway, a figure that is *almost* visible. The lure decides where the guest is looking, which means it decides where they are *not* looking.
2. **The hush.** Tension needs a vacuum. The music thins, the fog settles, the room goes quiet enough that the guest hears their own shoes. The hush is where dread grows, and it is the organ most builders cut first, because silence feels like nothing is happening. Nothing *is* happening. That is the point.
3. **The strike.** The payoff: the lunge, the drop, the shriek. It must come from the place the lure taught them *not* to watch.
4. **The release.** A laugh, a breath, a room that is merely creepy rather than hostile. Without release, the next strike lands on numb nerves.

### Where the timing lives

| Organ | Typical length | Who controls it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lure | 2–6 s | Room controller, on fixed cues |
| Hush | 3–12 s | The Director, within limits |
| Strike | under 1 s | Hard-wired cue, never the model |
| Release | 5–20 s | Performer, improvising |

Look hard at that third row. The strike is a fraction of a second of compressed air and noise, and it must fire *exactly* when the sensors say the guest is in the right spot — and never when anyone is somewhere it could hurt them. That is a job for a timer and a beam-break, not for a language model having a little think about it.[^hesitate]

The model earns its keep in the **hush** and the **release**: the parts of a scare where a live actor would improvise. How long to hold the silence. What to whisper afterwards. Whether to let the group go, or to follow the straggler with one more line. [The Director](section:the-director) is the system that makes those calls, and [Reading the Group](section:reading-the-group) is how it decides.

### A word for the jump scare

The cheap jump scare has a bad reputation, and it has earned it.[^cat] But a jump scare built on a properly long hush, struck from the blind side and released with a joke, is not cheap. It is carpentry. Read the history of the [jump scare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jump_scare) and you will find that the good ones were always about the *waiting*.

So when you sketch a room, sketch its four organs first, on paper, with a stopwatch in your other hand. Then — and only then — decide which of them deserves a robot.

[^hesitate]: I once watched a prototype hesitate for 1.4 seconds while it “decided” whether to lunge. The guest walked into the lunge. Nobody was hurt, because the arm had a soft stop and a padded face, and because I was standing beside the e-stop. Build as though you will not be that lucky.

[^cat]: Mostly in films, where the loud noise is usually a cat.
